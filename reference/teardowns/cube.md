# Cube (cubesoftware.com) teardown

Spreadsheet-native FP&A for mid-market finance teams. Read 2026-11-05 from public pages only (no trial, no form, no sign-in). The help center (help.cubesoftware.com), G2, Capterra, TrustRadius and The CFO Club refused direct fetches (403), so their content here comes through search-engine excerpts of those pages, and is marked as such. Vendor mockups are illustrative, not screenshots of a live tenant.

## 1. Product shape

**Who it is for.** Finance teams that already live in Excel or Google Sheets and will not leave them: CFOs, FP&A analysts, and the budget owners they report to. The homepage pitches "live, governed numbers in every tool your team uses" and names CFOs, FP&A analysts, CEOs, VPs of Sales, operations leaders and budget owners as users ([cubesoftware.com](https://www.cubesoftware.com/)). Typical contract is about $22K a year (Vendr median across 58 purchases, range $12.8K to $34.2K), historically $1,500 to $2,800 a month list ([Eightx review](https://eightx.co/blog/compare/reviews/cube-for-ecommerce-review)). Implementation 4 to 8 weeks (same source).

**The shape is a hub with many surfaces, not one app.** One governed data cube sits in the middle, and the numbers are served out to (per [cubesoftware.com](https://www.cubesoftware.com/)):
- **Cube web**, the workspace for planning, reporting, Boards (dashboards) and admin.
- **Excel and Google Sheets add-ins**, the primary working surface. Verbs are Fetch (pull current numbers into a template), Publish Data (push into a scenario), Publish Reports (save a range back to Cube for sharing), Drilldown (open the transactions behind a cell), and Validate (check the sheet's rows and columns map to Cube dimensions) ([help center via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/19622917563156)). The Excel add-in is Windows-only, Mac users go to Sheets or web ([Datarails comparison, competitor source](https://www.datarails.com/blog/cube-reviews-pros-and-cons-competitors/)).
- **PowerPoint and Google Slides**, where charts and tables are linked and "refresh in place" while keeping the deck's brand template ([executive reporting](https://www.cubesoftware.com/executive-reporting)).
- **Slack and Teams**, where an agent answers questions in threads.
- **AI assistants** through the Cube MCP Server, and BI tools (Tableau, Looker, Power BI) through governed exports and APIs ([reporting and analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/reporting-and-analysis)).

**Data model.** Four predefined dimensions (Account, Department, Scenario, Time) plus up to four custom ones such as Product or Vendor. Each is a hierarchy of members that roll up to parents in a mapped order. Account holds GL lines, formula accounts (Gross Profit) and operational metrics ("Sales per Rep"). Scenario holds versions (Actuals, Budget, Forecast). Time ranges are set at implementation and changed only through support, with rollup types controlling how months summarize to quarters and years ([Cube glossary via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052501091)). Boards filters additionally list Entity, Product and Market ([About Cube Boards via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/46398226692500-About-Cube-Boards)).

**AI layer (2025 to 2026).** Marketed as an "Agentic FP&A Platform" with four agent roles: Data Managers (collect, map, reconcile), Analysts (explain variances, flag anomalies), Planners (rerun forecasts, build scenarios) and Business Partners (deliver answers to department heads in Slack, Teams and email). The governing claim is that every AI insight "traces back to the transaction or assumption" ([cubesoftware.com](https://www.cubesoftware.com/)).

**Navigation.** No public documentation of the web app's nav shell was found. What is documented is navigation by verb inside each surface (Fetch, Publish, Drilldown in the add-in, Edit Mode versus view mode on Boards) and navigation by role (a "Viewing as David" permission view where each number follows the viewer's access, [cubesoftware.com](https://www.cubesoftware.com/)).

## 2. The financial dashboard

**Boards** is the current dashboard product inside Cube web (the older "Dashboards in workbooks" remain). Chart types: Bar, Line, Pie, Donut, Table, Metric, Trend, Row, Combo, Waterfall and Text. A widget is built by dragging dimensions into Rows, Columns and Filters, then picking a chart type. You can build from scratch, add a text block, or insert an existing widget from another board. Data refreshes on open or on a refresh button, never on a timer. Boards are not available inside the Excel or Sheets apps ([About Cube Boards via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/46398226692500-About-Cube-Boards)). Q2 2026 added a Visualization Agent that builds widgets from a prompt, time shortcuts and relative dates, board-level multi-currency filtering and drilldown from any widget ([2026 Product Updates via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/51048387237908-2026-Product-Updates)).

**What sits at the top (from Cube's own mockups).**
- A **headline card per question**, not a grid of tiles: "Opex · actual vs plan", a big "$1.55M", then "+$84K vs plan, explained", with tabs "Actuals" and "Forecast →" to switch basis ([variance analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis)).
- A **budget-versus-actual panel** "Budget vs. actual · Q3" with a "Live" tag, signed variance per line (cloud and software +$84K, contractors +$26K, marketing programs −$9K) and a commentary column beside the figures. The footer defines the math: "Variance = actual − plan, explained by driver" ([reporting and analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/reporting-and-analysis)).
- **KPI tiles** in board decks: "Q3 revenue $12.30M", "vs plan +2.1%", "NRR 112%" ([executive reporting](https://www.cubesoftware.com/executive-reporting)).

**KPIs Cube names.** Revenue against plan, gross margin, opex by department, opex as a percentage of revenue, cash and runway, headcount, net revenue retention, and "time to board pack" ([reporting and analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/reporting-and-analysis), [executive reporting](https://www.cubesoftware.com/executive-reporting)). Cube's own dashboard guide splits by audience: CFO (working capital, cash, net margin, recurring revenue, runway), P&L (revenue by stream or channel, opex by category, net income, NOI, COGS), cash flow (cash position at the top, then operating, investing and financing flows, net cash flow, cash conversion cycle), and budget versus actuals ([finance dashboard blog](https://www.cubesoftware.com/blog/finance-dashboard)).

**P&L, balance sheet, cash flow.** The board pack is fixed in order: summary, P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, KPIs against plan, variance commentary, outlook ([executive reporting](https://www.cubesoftware.com/executive-reporting)). The statements themselves are mostly spreadsheet templates fed by Fetch rather than designed web pages, which is the core of the product.

**Signature visual: the bridge.** "Q3 revenue bridge", subtitle "$12.30M, 2.1% ahead of plan", stamp "Updated 6:02 AM", waterfall bars Base, New, Exp., Churn, Q3 ([executive reporting](https://www.cubesoftware.com/executive-reporting)). The variance version decomposes into "= Plan $1.47M", "+ Price effect $61K", "+ Volume effect $31K", "− Timing $8K" in a panel called "Behind the variance" ([variance analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis)).

**Layout rules Cube preaches.** Most important at the top, trends in the middle, granular detail at the bottom. Number the visualizations so the board reads as a story. The "five-second rule": the main message lands in five seconds. Five to ten visualizations, past ten split the board. One rounding convention everywhere ([finance dashboard blog](https://www.cubesoftware.com/blog/finance-dashboard)). Pair related measures (liquidity with collections), show benchmark or target beside every primary metric ("Data is useless in isolation"), annotate why a number matters ([financial reporting dashboards blog](https://www.cubesoftware.com/blog/financial-reporting-dashboards)).

## 3. Interaction

- **Time.** Relative dates and time shortcuts on Boards since Q2 2026 ([2026 updates via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/51048387237908-2026-Product-Updates)). Monthly, quarterly and annual views of cash flow because "a trend invisible in one period may appear in another" ([blog](https://www.cubesoftware.com/blog/financial-reporting-dashboards)).
- **Comparisons.** Scenario is a dimension like any other, so actual versus budget, forecast or prior version is a filter or a column choice, shown in amount or percent ([reporting and analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/reporting-and-analysis)). A known AI trap: the Visualization Agent may pick Actuals when Budget was meant, and the help text tells users to name the scenario in the prompt ([Using AI to build widgets via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/48981044662036-Using-AI-to-Build-widgets-on-Boards)).
- **Drill-down.** The product's strongest idea. "Drilldown on every number" in web, sheets and decks. The drill panel for "Cloud and software · March, +$84.0K vs plan" shows driver rows first (Price effect +$61.5K, Headcount +$22.5K), then the invoices (date, vendor, description, amount, "CloudStack · annual renewal $184.0K"), then "+ 9 more invoices", footed "Source: ERP / GL" ([variance analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis)). Explanations carry a provenance chip like "Traced to 12 transactions" ([reporting and analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/reporting-and-analysis)). Q3 2026 made sheet drilldowns refreshable ranges, with a Dimensions / Transactions toggle when building one from scratch ([2026 updates via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/51048387237908-2026-Product-Updates)).
- **Formula transparency.** A "Behind every cell" panel spells out the arithmetic in words: "Q3 revenue =" recurring base + new business + expansion − churn, and price effect "(actual rate − plan rate) × actual volume" ([executive reporting](https://www.cubesoftware.com/executive-reporting), [variance analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis)).
- **Filters and segments.** Board filters are set in Edit Mode from Cube dimensions and a viewer's change updates every widget on the board ([About Cube Boards via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/46398226692500-About-Cube-Boards)). The older dashboards did not apply filters to every visual unless configured ([Get insights from dashboards via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/15766904250388-Get-insights-from-dashboards)). Slice by account, department or attribute "without leaving your report" ([variance analysis v1](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis-v1)).
- **Commentary.** Three layers. AI drafts commentary "next to each number" and finance reviews it before it ships ([variance analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis)). Users tag a department lead to explain a variance ([variance analysis v1](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis-v1)). Comments on web reports, board widgets and workflow tasks shipped Q2 2026 ([2026 updates via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/51048387237908-2026-Product-Updates)). Sample tone: "Cloud is over plan because two renewals landed early and DesignHub added 40 seats" ([reporting and analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/reporting-and-analysis)).
- **Exports and reports.** Linked decks with a Decks panel listing each object's state ("Revenue bridge · Refreshed", "KPI tiles · Refreshed", "Notes · Drafting") and one "Refresh all" button ([executive reporting](https://www.cubesoftware.com/executive-reporting)). Five-step close to board pack: close the books, link the deck, refresh, write the story, review and publish.
- **Audit and access.** An audit trail of human sentences ("Maya edited Q3 rent", "David approved forecast · 2:31 PM") with before and after values, and role views where each number follows the viewer ([cubesoftware.com](https://www.cubesoftware.com/), [reporting and analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/reporting-and-analysis)).

## 4. Visual language

Public sources show marketing mockups, not tenant screenshots, so this is what Cube chooses to present as its product, which is the useful part for us.
- **Typography as the hierarchy.** One very large figure per card, a mono-feeling label line above it with a middle dot separator ("Opex · actual vs plan", "Q3 revenue · slide 4"), and a sentence-case delta line below in words ("+$84K vs plan, explained"). Units are compact ($1.55M, $84K, $4,215.0K in drill detail, one decimal more precision as you go deeper).
- **Variance as signed text first, colour second.** Every variance carries an explicit + or − sign. Cube's own guidance is green favorable and red unfavorable, with blue and orange as the accessible pairing, and bright colour reserved for the insight that matters ([finance dashboard blog](https://www.cubesoftware.com/blog/finance-dashboard)). Favorable and unfavorable flags are described but not drawn in the mockups ([variance analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis)).
- **Source chips.** Small pills naming the system a figure came from: "ERP / GL Actuals", "CRM Bookings", "HRIS Headcount", "Plan FY budget" ([variance analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis)).
- **Status tags and timestamps.** "Live", "Refreshed", "Drafting", "Updated 6:02 AM", agent feed items stamped "6:04 AM". Freshness is always visible.
- **Density.** Low on the headline layer (a card answers one question), high on the drill layer (rows of invoices with dates and vendors). Five to ten widgets per board by their own rule.
- **Motion, empty and loading states.** Not documented publicly. The one documented behaviour is that refresh is explicit (on open or button), which avoids numbers shifting under a reader.

## 5. What makes it feel premium and useful

- **Every number answers "why" in one click.** The pitch line is literal: "The CFO asks why cloud and software is over plan. In Cube, the +$84K is one click from the invoices behind it" ([variance analysis](https://www.cubesoftware.com/variance-analysis)). Reviewers single this out: drill to transaction detail from Excel "significantly improved our variance analysis speed" ([Tooliverse aggregator, attribution uncertain](https://tooliverse.ai/tools/cube)).
- **The variance is decomposed, not just reported.** Plan, then price, volume and timing effects summing to actual, in plain English.
- **No new tool to learn.** Users keep their templates, which is the most consistently praised thing in reviews ([search summary of G2 and others](https://www.g2.com/products/cube-software/reviews/cube-software-review-9383700)). One reviewer's headline: "Super Intuitive, Best system if your business is not ready to shift from excel" (G2 via search).
- **One source of truth with an audit trail** removes duplicated spreadsheet updates ([Datarails, competitor](https://www.datarails.com/blog/cube-reviews-pros-and-cons-competitors/)).
- **Close to board pack in one refresh**, with each deck object's state visible.
- Strong scores: G2 4.5 of 5, Capterra 4.6, TrustRadius 9.6 of 10 ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/compare/reviews/cube-for-ecommerce-review)).

## 6. Complaints and weaknesses

- **Native dashboards are the weak part.** "Native dashboards are less deep", requests for more customization and visualization options, "more native visualization options within the web portal", and dashboarding "not intuitive enough compared with Power BI or Tableau" ([search summary](https://www.trustradius.com/products/cube-software/reviews?qs=6), [Tooliverse](https://tooliverse.ai/tools/cube), [G2 via search](https://www.g2.com/products/cube-software-cube/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons)). Many teams pair Cube with a separate BI tool.
- **Formatting does not persist.** "formatting of reports does not save in Cube", so users reformat or re-add accounts each pull ([G2 via search](https://g2.com/products/cube-software/reviews/cube-software-review-7816617)).
- **No suppress-zeroes option**, which makes detailed fetches tedious ([G2 via search](https://www.g2.com/products/cube-software-cube/reviews?page=3)).
- **Imports are slow and brittle.** 30 minutes to an hour a month on Intacct imports, and a NetSuite connector issue "still an ongoing issue after 8 months" ([Datarails](https://www.datarails.com/blog/cube-reviews-pros-and-cons-competitors/), [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/compare/reviews/cube-for-ecommerce-review)).
- **Dimension cap.** Four custom dimensions. Datarails claims this came up in 54% of the sales calls it won (competitor source, treat with care). Time ranges cannot be edited without support ([glossary via search](https://help.cubesoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052501091)).
- **Interface can be clunky**, implementation needs trial and error, few tutorials ([G2 summary via search](https://g2.com/products/cube-software/reviews?page=2)).
- **Ad hoc reporting is hard** because everything rides on Excel templates whose formulas and links must be double-checked (G2 via search).
- **Platform gaps:** Excel add-in Windows-only, no native Shopify connector, no SKU-level inventory modeling, thin headcount planning ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/compare/reviews/cube-for-ecommerce-review)).
- **AI picks the wrong scenario** unless told (help center's own warning, above).

## 7. Steal list (for Hub's financial tab)

1. **Headline card grammar:** a label line with a middle dot ("Gross margin · actual vs budget"), one large figure, then a signed delta sentence ending in a link-word ("+$84K vs budget, explained") that opens the drill.
2. **Drill drawer in two tiers:** clicking any figure opens a panel with driver rows first (price, volume, timing, or by job or store), then the source transactions with date, vendor, memo and amount, a "+ N more" row and a "Source: QuickBooks GL" footer.
3. **"Behind the variance" bridge:** every budget variance renders as Plan → named effects → Actual, as both a small waterfall and the same arithmetic written in one plain-English line.
4. **Provenance chips on every figure:** a small pill naming the system and freshness ("GL actuals · updated 6:02 AM", "Budget FY26") so trust is visible without a footnote.
5. **Scenario as a first-class toggle:** Actual, Budget, Forecast and Prior year as tabs on the card itself, with the basis always named in the label so a number can never be read against the wrong comparison.
6. **Commentary sits in a column beside the numbers:** a draft sentence per material line ("Over budget because two renewals landed early"), marked Draft until a person approves, with the approver and time shown.
7. **A refresh-state panel for exports:** the board-pack or PDF export lists each section with a status (Refreshed, Drafting, Stale) and one Refresh all button, mirroring Cube's Decks panel.
8. **One fixed statement order and one rounding rule:** summary, P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, KPIs against plan, commentary, outlook, with compact units on headline cards and one more decimal in the drill.

## 8. Avoid list

1. **Making the spreadsheet the product.** Cube's statements live in user templates, which is why formatting drifts, ad hoc reports are hard and the web dashboards are thin. Hub should design the statements as pages.
2. **A widget builder as the main dashboard experience.** Drag-dimensions-into-Rows-and-Columns is the part reviewers call less deep than Power BI. Give SMB owners a finished, opinionated financial tab, with configuration in settings.
3. **Dead zero rows and a hard dimension cap.** Suppress zero lines by default and do not cap segments (job, store, company) at a small fixed count.
4. **AI that guesses the comparison basis.** Never let generated narrative or charts infer Actual versus Budget silently: the basis is chosen by the user and printed on the output.
