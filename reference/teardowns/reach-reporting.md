# Reach Reporting teardown

Researched 2026-11-05 from public sources only (no trial, no forms). Reach Reporting is report and dashboard software for accountants, fractional CFOs and small businesses: it connects to the ledger, then lays out branded report packs and dashboards from templates. ⚠️ Every screenshot on the marketing and help pages came back as an empty image placeholder, so the visual-language notes below come from captions and settings documentation. Where a source says nothing, this file says so too.

## 1. Product shape

- **Who it's for:** accounting firms, advisors and CFOs, small businesses, franchises and nonprofits ([home](https://reachreporting.com)). It is sold per ledger connection: $149/month for 1, $290 for 10, $550 for 25, with unlimited users and deliverables on every plan, and white-label branding at extra cost ([pricing](https://www.reachreporting.com/pricing)).
- **Data in:** QuickBooks Online and Desktop, Xero, Digits, MYOB, Gusto, Google Sheets, Excel, and a CSV trial balance ([home](https://reachreporting.com), [knowledge center](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/new-features-and-updates)).
- **Main objects:** a Workspace holds companies. Each company has Dashboards, Reports, Metrics (the building block), Sheets (custom data and formulas), Budgets & Forecasts (a 3-way model), Consolidations and a Client Portal ([knowledge center](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com)).
- **Navigation:** open a company in the Workspace, then pick a module from icons in a left rail. Dashboards can be starred as favourites, and a new one starts from a saved template, a pre-built template, a blank build, or AI generation ([dashboard overview](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/reach-reporting-dashboard-overview)).
- **The core idea:** everything is a **Metric**, a small spreadsheet (columns are dates, budget, classes or formulas, rows are accounts or custom rows) that renders as a table or a chart. Dashboards and reports are canvases of Metrics, Statements, Live Text, text, shapes and images ([metric builder](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/building-metrics-and-statements-with-reach-reporting), [report builder](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/using-the-report-builder)).

## 2. The financial dashboard

- **Layout:** a grid of movable, resizable tiles. Drag a tile and the rest reflow. Resize from a lower-right handle. "Add Metric" offers Templates, Statements, Quick Build, Dividers and Live Text ([dashboard overview](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/reach-reporting-dashboard-overview)). Dividers suggest dashboards are organised as labelled bands rather than one undifferentiated grid.
- **What sits at the top:** the marketing hero shows "KPIs, income trends and cash flow" on one dashboard ([home](https://reachreporting.com)). In practice the top is whatever the template puts there, typically Live Text figures (a value or a sentence) above charts.
- **Built-in ratios:** Gross Profit Margin, Net Profit Margin, Working Capital, Current Ratio, Net Assets, EBIT, EBITDA and their margins (EBIT and EBITDA need configuring in company settings first) ([data picker](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/explaining-ratios/metrics-in-the-data-picker)).
- **Ranked lists ("Special Metrics"):** Top Customers, Top Vendors, Top Income Accounts, Top COGS, Top Expenses, with parent or sub-account depth ([special metrics](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/special-metrics)).
- **Statements:** P&L, balance sheet, cash flow and AR/AP aging follow the client's own chart of accounts and stay "locked" so they update as accounts are added, deleted or merged. Sections collapse and expand, zero rows hide, rows sort, extra date or formula columns are added, and long statements paginate with titles and disclaimers repeated on every page ([statements](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/using-statements-in-reach-reporting)). An "unlocked" statement can be edited freely, and a statement can be converted into a Metric ([metric builder index](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/metrics-statement-builder)).
- **Chart types:** Table, Column, Line, Bar, Scatter, Pie, with mixed types per data series (the combo chart), stacked column and bar, a right Y axis, min and max Y, value labels, legends, and **goal lines** on everything except tables and pies ([metric builder](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/building-metrics-and-statements-with-reach-reporting)). No waterfall, gauge or sparkline is documented.
- **Budget columns:** the "Actuals vs Budget/Forecast (relative to book month)" column type shows actuals for closed months and budget for the rest, so one series rolls from history into plan as the book month advances. Forecast columns get a distinct chart style (a "Styles for Forecast Columns" checkbox), and the budget's label can be renamed (for example "Plan") ([actual vs budget](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/actual-vs-budget-column-type)).

## 3. Interaction

- **Time:** a dashboard-wide "Last Closed Month" dropdown that reaches back to the book month set in company settings ([dashboard overview](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/reach-reporting-dashboard-overview)). Live Text takes rolling presets (Last Month, Year to Date, Trailing 12) or a custom range, always relative to the book month, so a report opened later still shows the right period ([live text](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/live-text)). Individual tiles can carry their own date dropdown ("Date Drop-down Ability") ([metric builder index](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/metrics-statement-builder)).
- **The book month as the clock:** the whole product is anchored to one company setting, the last closed month, rather than today's date. That is the reason templates roll forward without being rebuilt.
- **Comparisons:** Actual, Budget/Forecast, Actual vs Budget/Forecast and Actual Comparisons (period against period), with amount or percent change ([live text](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/live-text)). Smart Formula Columns add calculated columns ([statements](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/using-statements-in-reach-reporting)).
- **Filters and segments:** class, department or location applies to the whole dashboard in one click, and a tile can override it ("Class/Dept Filter Override") ([dashboard overview](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/reach-reporting-dashboard-overview)). Class or department columns can be added to a Metric in one click ([metric builder](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/building-metrics-and-statements-with-reach-reporting)). ⚠️ Ratios built in a Sheet ignore the class filter and use company totals ([live text](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/live-text)), a quiet correctness trap.
- **Row Change:** a custom row becomes a viewer-facing dropdown of named scenarios, and picking one recalculates the metric, in the portal as well as in reports ([row change](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/display-options-row-change)). This is a light what-if control handed to the client.
- **Drill-down:** the marketing page names drill-down by class and department and by YTD, month, quarter and year ([storytelling](https://www.reachreporting.com/feature-storytelling)), and consolidations drill from consolidated to company level ([consolidations](https://www.reachreporting.com/feature-consolidations)). Drill from a figure to its transactions is **not documented** anywhere I found.
- **Entities:** consolidations with automatic intercompany eliminations, selective eliminations for currency differences, historical-rate currency translation and FCTR rows ([consolidations](https://www.reachreporting.com/feature-consolidations)).
- **Commentary and narrative:** two mechanisms. **Live Text** is a figure embedded in prose that updates with the ledger, shown as `{Total Income}` while editing and highlighted blue to set it apart from static text. It can render as a bare value or as a generated sentence ("Use Live Sentence"), abbreviates ($1.2M), shows negatives in parentheses, and carries up and down colours and icons on comparisons ([live text](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/live-text), [conditional formatting](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/conditional-formatting)). **AI Insights** writes narrative on trends, anomalies and risks, with alerts by app or email ([AI page](https://www.reachreporting.com/ai-financial-reporting)), and Claude-built AI widgets are generated inside dashboards under a monthly usage cap ([Claude widgets](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/claude-ai-dashboard-widgets)). How AI output is reviewed or edited before a client sees it is not documented.
- **Exports and delivery:** Excel export keeps the current filters and period but drops conditional-formatting icons, combo charts and chart colours. PDF is a snapshot that resizes to the page, and long or scrolling dashboards "may print poorly" ([dashboard overview](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/reach-reporting-dashboard-overview)). Publishing gives a link with Preview, Copy, optional password and Embed (with a theme choice), or a Client Portal tile. The owner decides which controls a client gets (month selection, export, filters, AI). Viewers get a non-editable copy that updates when the original changes, and unpublishing cuts every viewer off at once ([publishing](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/how-to-publish-dashboards)).
- **Reports:** a page-by-page canvas with page duplication, reordering, a background colour per page, a table of contents, portrait or landscape, and a cross-company **Scratchpad** whose saved pages and metrics re-point to whichever company is open ([report builder](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/using-the-report-builder), [reports index](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/reports)). Cash and accrual versions of one report are supported ([reports index](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/reports)).

## 4. Visual language

- **Colour:** one theme colour per report, picked by hex. Only elements explicitly set to "Use Theme Color" follow it, and a separate company theme colour governs metrics and dashboards ([theme colour](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/theme-color-in-the-report-builder)). No derived palette or tints are documented, so a brand is one accent plus hand-set series colours ([metric colours](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/metrics-statement-builder)).
- **Type:** font, size, colour and alignment from a top toolbar, per text box. No house type scale is documented ([report builder](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/using-the-report-builder)).
- **Density:** tables offer striped rows, column lines and hidden zero rows ([statements](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/using-statements-in-reach-reporting)). That is accountant density: a statement looks like a statement.
- **Status and variance:** user-built conditional formatting rules on cells, rows or columns, compared with other columns or fixed values, plus a colour and icon for "if up" and "if down" on Live Text ([conditional formatting](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/conditional-formatting)). ⚠️ The direction is "up" and "down", not "good" and "bad", so a rising expense turning green is the user's problem to configure. A rule cannot compare non-contiguous cells.
- **Motion:** none documented beyond the tile reflow while dragging.
- **Empty and loading states:** not documented in any source I could read.
- **Responsive:** published dashboards adapt to phones and tablets ([publishing](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/how-to-publish-dashboards)).

The honest read: Reach's look is whatever the accountant builds from templates. It is a layout tool with good defaults, not an opinionated design system.

## 5. What feels premium, what is useful

- **Premium:** the client never sees a spreadsheet. They get a branded pack with their logo, a table of contents, numbers woven into sentences, and a live link or a portal with a password. The firm's brand rides on it, and white-label removes Reach's ([storytelling](https://www.reachreporting.com/feature-storytelling), [pricing](https://www.reachreporting.com/pricing)).
- **Useful, the thing users love:** templates that roll forward. G2 reviewers say templates make setup take "minutes vs days" and praise support ([G2 via search](https://www.g2.com/products/reach-reporting/reviews)). The book month anchor, locked statements and Live Text mean next month's pack is already written. One Capterra reviewer valued uploading a trial balance for clients on outdated ledgers ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/184036/Reach-Reporting/reviews/)). Ratings sit at 4.9 on both G2 (27 reviews) and Capterra.

## 6. Complaints and weaknesses

- **Learning curve on customising** dashboards and reports, "bigger than other reporting software", and one 3-star CFO said onboarding was solo or by video, so they never got past basic use ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/reviews/184036/Reach-Reporting), [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/finance-accounting-software/a/reach-reporting/reviews/)).
- **Formatting is clunky:** "a bit clunky and hard to use", and report-item formatting "could be a bit more functional" (same sources).
- **AI widgets fail on projections** (same sources).
- **No small text box or notice on a dashboard** was a named wish (same sources), which says commentary on dashboards is weak compared with reports.
- **Export loses fidelity:** Excel drops icons, combo charts and colours, and PDFs of long dashboards print poorly ([dashboard overview](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/reach-reporting-dashboard-overview)).
- **It only shows what the ledger holds:** a late close means a stale pack, and it does nothing about the data assembly upstream ([Futureproof](https://www.runfutureproof.com/blog/client-reporting-software)).
- **Gaps by connector:** Top Customers and Top Vendors are missing for Xero, MYOB and consolidations ([special metrics](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/special-metrics)), and fund-accounting systems are absent.

## 7. Steal list

1. **Anchor every figure to the closed month, not today:** one company-level "book month" that every preset (Last Month, YTD, T12) resolves against, so the financial tab is always correct on open and a tentative month is shown as such.
2. **Live sentences:** a variance headline written as a sentence whose figures are live tokens, abbreviated ($1.2M), negatives in parentheses, and coloured by direction, so the top of the P&L reads as an answer before a chart.
3. **One series that rolls from actual into plan:** a single line or column series that is solid for closed months and styled as forecast (hatched or dashed) for the rest, with the budget label renamable ("Plan").
4. **Goal lines on every chart** (margin target, budget, covenant floor) drawn as a labelled horizontal rule, never on tables or pies.
5. **One segment filter for the whole tab, with a per-panel override:** pick a job, store or class once and every panel follows, while a panel that must stay company-wide says so with a small pinned badge.
6. **Ranked lists as a first-class panel:** top customers, vendors, income, COGS and expense accounts, with a parent or sub-account toggle and share of total beside each row.
7. **A viewer-facing scenario dropdown (Row Change):** a named set of values (base, downside, upside growth) a client can flip on the page to recalculate one panel, without opening a planning tool.
8. **Viewer controls set per audience:** the owner decides whether a given viewer level may change the month, filter, export or see AI notes, which maps straight onto Hub's eight view levels.

## 8. Avoid list

1. **Up and down instead of good and bad.** Variance colour must know the account's sign (a rising expense is red), set once by the system, never left to each user's rule.
2. **A single theme colour with no derived palette.** Hub already derives its colours from one brand colour, so keep that and do not regress to hand-picked series colours per chart.
3. **Snapshot exports that lose the design.** If Hub exports, the PDF and workbook carry the same colours, icons and layout as the page, and long statements paginate on purpose.
4. **A blank-canvas builder as the main path.** Reach's learning-curve complaints come from asking accountants to design. Hub should ship an opinionated financial tab and expose configuration, not a drag-and-drop canvas.
